change control: the operator's clock, frozen evidence, a formal document, and a gate on the button people actually press

Four defects, none of which ever raised an exception.

1. The maintenance window was stored in the wrong timezone. Every timestamp was
   DISPLAYED through settings_store.to_local while every form value was STORED
   as if the operator had typed UTC. A window entered as 22:00 on a
   Europe/Zurich console opened at midnight local - two hours after the customer
   had been told the outage would start. The schedule and the notice agreed with
   each other and both disagreed with the human. settings_store.parse_local is
   the missing inverse; the load-bearing guard asserts the ROUND TRIP, because a
   conversion wrong in both directions is perfectly self-consistent.

   Wall-clock schedules had the same bug at fleet scale: "every night at 02:00"
   computed in UTC fired at 03:00 in winter and 04:00 in summer. compute_next_run
   now takes tz for daily/weekly/monthly - passed DOWN, so services/scheduler
   stays pure schedule math (guarded: no settings_store, no models, no db there,
   and an AST guard that every caller passes tz=). interval is a duration and
   once is already an absolute instant; converting either would double-apply.

2. The pre-upgrade left no evidence. upgrade.prepare() painted its result into
   the browser and vanished with the tab, so "pre-upgrade passed, therefore
   raise the change" had nothing to attach. Runs are persisted (UpgradePrep) and
   append-only: an approved change cites a specific run, and a row that can be
   overwritten in place is not evidence. The verdict grades only the sections
   that were REQUESTED, and an unreachable published service is a BASELINE, not
   a failure - finding a policy already down before the change is the most
   valuable thing the pre-flight produces, and grading it red would train people
   to re-run until it turns green.

3. The affected-service inventory was read live at render time, so an approved
   change and the change that ran could describe different customers. It is
   frozen onto the change when raised; drift against the devices now is
   REPORTED, never merged.

4. Change control only bound the unused code path. The headless executor had
   refused to flash outside a window since the gate was generalised, but the
   button a human clicks went straight to push_firmware with nothing but
   CONFIG_WRITE and a typed device name. A live flash now needs an approved,
   open window; the refusal names the closest candidate and why. Dry runs are
   never gated - they send nothing, and gating them would push operators to skip
   validation. The change is opened and closed through ONE seam, not by stamping
   the worker's six terminal paths, because that is how one gets missed.

Also: a formal 13-section change document in English or German. The German text
describes what a Fortinet upgrade actually is - a firmware image uploaded by
REST, then a reboot into the target partition - and never operating-system
patching, guarded by a forbidden-phrase list: a document describing work that
does not happen is worse than none, because the approver signs the wrong thing.
custom_rest prints the literal call and refuses to estimate an impact it cannot
know. Section 12 prints the one approval SATOM records and leaves the other
roles blank rather than fabricating them.

The inventory exports to .xlsx or .csv with chosen columns, in fixed catalog
order so two exports of one change are comparable. The xlsx writer is pure
standard library: the product ships offline bundles, and a binary dependency is
a cost every installation pays forever.

Verified: targeted tests only (no full suite, per standing instruction) -
test_cr_timezone_evidence (53), test_cr_document (148), test_xlsx_writer (33)
plus the CR, appliance, scheduler, docs and site neighbours, all rc=0. 22
mutations, 22 bite - the first pass left the upgrade gate's own decision
function untested (neutering it broke nothing) and that hole is now closed with
ten behavioural guards. 56 live checks against a1's real database and a real
admin session, 0 failures, no appliance contacted, throwaway rows removed.
